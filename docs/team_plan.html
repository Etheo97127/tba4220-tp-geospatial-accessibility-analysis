Whose 15-Minute City?

Team plan: four tracks, six phases, one target question

The proposal asks one thing: what kinds of neighbourhood exist in Singapore, and does the same set appear in towns planned in different eras? This plan splits that work into four tracks and orders it into phases. The access-score track carries 40% of the effort because every question runs on its output. Each question track carries 20%.

Ground rules

One row per residential subzone

Every table in the project has the same rows. When any track says “subzone”, this is what it means.

Unit
URA Master Plan 2019 subzone: 332 in total, nested in 55 planning areas and 5 regions. File: data/raw/sg/mp19_subzone.geojson. Census 2020 is published on the same boundaries.
Which subzones count
The residential set (H2): subzones above the population cut-off fixed in P0. Airports, reservoirs, ports and industrial estates drop out. C1 publishes the list and its n.
Standardising
Whenever scores are rescaled (category scores in A5, z-scores for k-means in D2), the mean, SD or maximum comes from the residential set only, so empty subzones do not set the scale.
Join key and CRS
subzone_c (e.g. BMSZ12), never the name. EPSG:3414 (SVY21, metres) for every layer.
Where data lives
In the repo. Raw downloads go in data/raw/sg/: committed, never edited, one row each in docs/data_log.md. Lookups go in data/external/, hand-offs in data/processed/. data/interim/ is git-ignored and rebuilt by script, as is any file over ~50 MB.

    Glossary

    Terms used in this plan

    Plain-language meanings. In the methods table, “Taught” says whether the lectures covered it. Anything marked “No” needs a citation and a one-line explanation in the report.

    Planning, housing and project terms

    TermMeaning
    SubzoneSmallest URA planning unit, from a few streets to a whole estate (median 1.2 km²). One subzone is one row in every table.
    Planning areaA group of subzones, e.g. Bedok or Toa Payoh; 55 in total. Used as the starting resampling blocks.
    HDB townHDB’s own grouping of public-housing estates. Close to planning areas, but the boundaries are not identical.
    Mature / non-mature townHDB’s old label. Mature towns were built earlier and have established amenities (e.g. Toa Payoh, Queenstown); non-mature towns are newer and still filling in (e.g. Punggol, Sengkang). HDB retired the label in 2024; we use it as a fixed historical grouping.
    Residential subzoneA subzone with enough residents for its score to mean something. The cut-off is fixed in P0 and applied in C1.
    Elderly shareShare of a subzone’s residents aged 65 and over (Census 2020). Q2 expects a higher elderly share to go with a lower score.
    Housing mixThe kinds of home residents live in, from the Census 2020 “type of dwelling” table: HDB 1–2 room, 3-room, 4-room, 5-room and executive flats, condominiums and other apartments, landed houses. It stands in for wealth, because income is not published by subzone. Reduced to one number, e.g. share in HDB 1–3 room flats (higher means less affluent).
    Population densityResidents per km² of subzone area. A control in Q2: shops go where people are.
    Distance to transitDistance from the population-weighted centre to the nearest MRT or LRT station.
    Access scoreHow many everyday facilities lie within 1 km of where a subzone’s residents live, per category and combined.
    CategoryOne of six service groups: food services, retail goods, health, food retail, education, recreation.
    HES weightsEach category’s share of household spending in the 2023 Household Expenditure Survey. Sets how much a category counts in the composite.
    Composite scoreThe six category scores combined using the HES weights.
    VarietyHow many different kinds of facility (distinct OSM tags) are within reach. Counted separately from the score.
    Population-weighted centreThe point where a subzone’s residents live on average, rather than its geometric middle.
    15-minute cityThe planning idea that daily needs should be a short walk or ride from home. Singapore’s version is “20-minute towns” (LTMP 2040).
    Under-servedA subzone or type whose score is lower than Q2’s model predicts for it (a negative residual).
    OSMOpenStreetMap: a free, volunteer-built world map and our source of facility locations.
    CrosswalkLookup table from OSM tags (e.g. amenity=clinic) to our six categories.
    ECDA registerThe official list of licensed preschools. Used only to check how complete OSM is.
    Recall / coverageShare of ECDA preschools that OSM also contains.
    OSM contributor densityDistinct OSM mappers per km²: a stand-in for how much mapping effort an area has had.
    Hand-offA table one track gives another, keyed by subzone_c (H1–H8).

    Methods

    TermMeaningTaught
    CRS / SVY21Coordinate reference system. SVY21 (EPSG:3414) is Singapore’s, in metres, so distances come out in metres.Lecture 3
    Buffer / within-distanceEverything within a set distance of a point; how the 1 km count is done.Lectures 4–5
    Spatial autocorrelationNearby places having similar values.Lecture 8
    Spatial weightsThe rule for who counts as a neighbour. Queen: shares a border or a corner. kNN: the k nearest centres.Lecture 8 (queen); kNN no
    Moran’s IOne number for a whole map. Positive: similar values cluster. Near 0: random. Negative: high next to low, like a checkerboard.Lecture 8
    Permutation testShuffle the values many times (e.g. 999) and see how often chance gives a result as strong as the real one. That share is the p-value.Lecture 8
    LISALocal Moran’s I: Moran’s I for each subzone and its neighbours. Labels clusters (High-High, Low-Low) and outliers (High-Low, Low-High), the four quadrants of the Moran scatterplot.No
    Getis-Ord Gi*For each subzone, compares the total of it and its neighbours with the city-wide average. Finds hot and cold spots, but not outliers.No
    Benjamini-HochbergA multiple-testing correction. With 300 tests at p < 0.05, about 15 pass by chance; this adjusts the p-values so they don’t.No
    OLSOrdinary least squares: standard linear regression, lm() in R. Fits the line that makes squared prediction errors smallest.No
    CoefficientHow much the score changes when one predictor rises by one unit (or one SD), with the others held fixed.No
    One-sided testTests for an effect in one stated direction only, e.g. elderly share lowers the score.No
    VIFVariance inflation factor. Flags predictors that overlap so much that their effects cannot be told apart (above ~5 is a warning).No
    ResidualActual score minus the score the model predicts.No
    R²Share of the variation in the score that the model explains (0 to 1).No
    LM testsLagrange multiplier tests, run on OLS residuals. They say whether leftover spatial pattern looks like a lag (a score depends on its neighbours’ scores) or an error (unmeasured causes cluster in space), which picks the spatial model.No
    Spatial error modelRegression that allows the errors of neighbouring subzones to be correlated. Fixes OLS p-values that are too small.No
    z-scoreValue minus the mean, divided by the standard deviation. Puts categories on one scale before clustering.—
    k-meansSplits subzones into k groups, each as close as possible to its group’s average profile.Lecture 4 (as a map class style only)
    Elbow / silhouetteWays to choose k. Elbow: the point where more groups stop helping much. Silhouette: how well each subzone fits its own group compared with the next nearest.No
    Spatial block bootstrapRerun the clustering many times on data resampled in whole blocks of neighbouring subzones, and see which groups survive.No
    Jaccard stabilityOverlap between a cluster and its match in a bootstrap run, from 0 to 1. Above 0.75 is usually called stable.No
